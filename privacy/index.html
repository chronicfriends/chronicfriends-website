Privacy Policy

Privacy Policy for Chronic Friends
Last updated: 9 November 2026 · Version: 3.7

Chronic Friends is a wellness, self-tracking, and community app for people living with chronic illness. Protecting your data matters deeply to us — especially because we handle information about your health. This Privacy Policy explains, in plain language, what data we collect, why we use it, who we share it with, and what rights you have.

Where you can use Chronic Friends. Chronic Friends is offered in two places, and this policy covers both. There is the mobile app for iPhone and Android, and there is the web version at chronicfriends.app, which runs in your browser on a computer and can be installed as a desktop app. Both are the same product: you sign in with the same account, and your journal, community and settings are the same data, synchronised through your account. Four things work differently in the browser: Health Sync is not available (it reads from Apple Health or Health Connect, which only exist on your phone); medication reminders only ring while the page is open, because a browser cannot wake itself up; and Premium cannot be purchased from a computer — it is activated in the mobile app, and a subscription bought there unlocks the web version too; and an account created with «Continue with Apple» or «Continue with Google» can, for now, only be used in the mobile app, because the web version does not offer those two buttons yet. Our public information website, chronicfriends.org, is a separate site and does not require an account.

Our mission is to help people all over the world who live with a chronic condition feel less alone, understand their own patterns, and carry their health information with confidence. Chronic Friends is dedicated exclusively to chronic illness and supports a broad and growing range of conditions — digestive, musculoskeletal, autoimmune, respiratory, metabolic, neurological, pelvic, and mental-health conditions, among others (see our Terms of Service for the current list). We do not privilege any single "primary" condition: every condition is treated equally, and whatever condition brought you here, the way we protect your data is exactly the same.

Chronic Friends is available to users around the world. We use the GDPR (EU) and the revFADP (Switzerland) as our global baseline, because they are among the strictest data protection laws in the world. For users in the United States, we also follow the U.S. rules that apply to consumer health apps, such as the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule and state health-privacy laws (for example California's CCPA/CPRA and Washington's My Health My Data Act). We also aim to comply with the data protection laws that apply where you live. Depending on your country or region, you may have additional rights (see Section 8).

1. What data we collect

  • Account / profile: email, username, encrypted password, language, country, your age, and the optional short bio you may write about yourself. Age is asked because Chronic Friends is for adults only; the bio is free text and you decide whether to write one.
  • Signing in with Apple or Google (optional): if you tap «Continue with Apple» or «Continue with Google» instead of using an email and a password, Apple or Google confirm who you are and share with us your email address and an identifier for your account. If you sign in with Apple and choose «Hide My Email», the address we receive is a private one from Apple that forwards our emails to you — it is the one you will see in Settings. Google also shares the name and profile picture of your Google Account: our sign-in service (Firebase) keeps them with your sign-in details, but the app does not use them or show them to anyone. If Apple or Google share your first name, the app only uses it to fill in «What should we call you?» when you set up your profile, and you can keep it or change it. We never receive your Apple or Google password, and an account created this way has no Chronic Friends password.
  • Health & wellness data (special category): the condition(s) you choose to follow and the number of years you have lived with them; your weight and height, if you enter them; the medications you add (name, dose, schedule and how many you have left) and each dose you mark as taken, skipped or postponed; the appointments you note down; what you log in Flare mode; symptom and wellness check-ins; journal entries and notes about how you feel; food logging and scanning, and the foods you mark as tolerated or not; and the parameters you enter for self-tracking and visualization — including body-map entries, the inputs shown in the symptom/wellness radar, energy and fatigue logs, and any readings you type in yourself from your own home devices (for example blood pressure, peak flow, or glucose). All of this is data you enter yourself — the only exception is Health Sync, described next, which reads from your phone's own health store if you switch it on.
  • Emergency & insurance card (optional): if you fill in this card, it is stored in your account so that you have it on any device: your name, date of birth, blood type, condition, current medication, allergies, an emergency contact (the name and phone number of another person — please let them know), your health insurer and policy number, your doctor and, if you add one, a photo of your physical card. The photo is stored in Frankfurt with your other files (Section 5). Only you can see the card.
  • Health Sync — readings from your phone's health store (optional, off until you turn it on): if you enable Health Sync and grant permission in your phone's own system dialog, the app reads your step count, sleep and activity minutes from Apple Health (HealthKit) on iPhone or Health Connect on Android — and, on iPhone only, also the stages of your sleep and, if your device records them, your resting heart rate and heart-rate variability. Three things about this: it is read-only (we never write anything into Apple Health or Health Connect); each day it runs, it saves that day’s steps, sleep and activity minutes in your journal, just as if you had typed them in yourself, so they are stored in your account with the rest of your journal (see Section 5) and can appear in a PDF report that you choose to generate, while resting heart rate and heart-rate variability stay only on your device and are never uploaded; and if a day has no data, the app says so instead of estimating a value. Other members never see any of these readings — the only exception is the steps tournament, if you join it, which shows the four step totals described below and nothing else. You can withdraw this permission at any time from your phone's settings, and the rest of the app keeps working exactly the same.
  • The steps tournament (optional, off until you join): Chronic Friends includes a friendly steps tournament — a table of who has walked the most today, this week, this month and this year, which starts again on 1 January. Taking part is a choice you make, and nothing is published until you make it. If you join, four numbers — your step totals for those four periods — are uploaded to our servers together with the display name and avatar you already show in the community, so that other members can see them in the table. Nothing else about your health goes with them: not sleep, not activity minutes, not a single day-by-day figure, and nothing from your journal. If you leave the tournament, your row is deleted, not hidden, and you disappear from the table.
  • Self-assessment questionnaires (optional): the answers you give to standardised questionnaires you choose to complete — such as the PHQ-9, the GAD-7, the AUDIT-C or a condition activity index — and the score they add up to. You choose whether to open one. Your answers and scores are kept only on your device: they are not uploaded to your account, so they do not come back if you change phone or computer. There is not yet a way to delete a single result; they are removed if you delete the app, or if you remove your data from a computer when you log out. These questionnaires are reproduced from their official published versions and the app shows you the score without interpreting it (see Section 11).
  • Community: your posts, your comments and your private messages. If you add a photo to a post, the app re-saves it on your device before uploading it, and that removes the hidden details stored inside the photo — including the location (GPS) where it was taken.
  • Photo diary (optional): the photos you take in the Photo diary — for example, of your skin — are kept only on your device. They are never uploaded to our servers or to your account, so they do not come back if you change phone or computer, and they are lost if you delete the app or remove your data from a computer when you log out.
  • Reminders and notifications: medication reminders, the daily check-in reminder and the app’s other notifications are scheduled on your own device. They do not go through our servers or through Apple’s or Google’s notification services, and the app does not send notifications about new messages. On the lock screen, a medication reminder does not show the name of the medicine unless you switch on «Show medication names on the lock screen».
  • Together mode (optional): if you invite a friend to do an activity at the same time as you, or accept their invitation, our servers keep a small record of that session: the two account identifiers, the activity, whether it is invited, live or ended, and when each of you was last connected. It contains no health data, and it is deleted when either of you deletes their account.
  • Doctor accounts and consultations: online consultations with doctors are not available yet, so no consultation, payment or doctor-verification data is collected today. If you create a doctor account, the professional details you enter — such as your speciality, licence number, schedule and payment details — are kept only on your device. Before consultations start, we will update this policy to describe them and to name the providers involved.
  • Subscription / payment: subscription status via RevenueCat; charges via Apple / Google. We never store card details.
  • Technical / usage: device, operating system, app version, identifiers, and error reports. On the phone, if the app crashes or something fails, Firebase Crashlytics receives a technical report: what failed and where in the code, the device model, the operating system, the app version and an identifier for that installation of the app. We do not add your account, your name or anything you write in the app to these reports. The web version does not send error reports.

2. How we use your data

We use your data to: run your account; provide the health and self-tracking features; run the community and messaging, and keep it safe by handling reports and blocks; manage subscriptions; keep the app secure; find and fix errors; improve it; send you important service notices; and, only if you agree, send you tips about using the app and an occasional request for a review.

Artificial intelligence. This version of Chronic Friends does not send your health data to any artificial-intelligence service. Nothing you log is transmitted to a third-party AI provider, and no AI feature is active in the app.

If we ever introduce a feature that does send your data to an AI provider, we will not switch it on silently. We will first update this policy to name the provider, the data involved, where it is processed and how long it is kept, and we will ask you for separate, explicit consent before a single entry leaves your device. You will be able to withdraw that consent at any time, and refusing it will never block the rest of the app. Any such feature would be informational only: it would describe your own logged data and would not predict, diagnose, or recommend treatment (see Section 11).

We never sell your data and never use it for advertising.

3. Legal basis

  • Explicit consent (Art. 9.2.a GDPR) for health data — you can withdraw it at any time by deleting your account in Settings › «Delete my account», which erases your health data, or by writing to us (Section 8).
  • Consent (Art. 6.1.a) for the emails with tips and review requests — you can withdraw it at any time (Section 4).
  • Contract (Art. 6.1.b) for your account and your subscription.
  • Legitimate interest (Art. 6.1.f) for security, error reports, keeping the community safe (reports and blocks), listing you in the member directory (which you can switch off at any time, Section 4) and improvement.
  • Legal obligation (Art. 6.1.c) where the law requires it.

4. Who we share with

  • Firebase (Google) — sign-in (authentication), database, file storage, our server functions and, on the phone, error reports (Crashlytics). Firebase also sends the email to reset your password. Section 5 explains where each of them is located.
  • Brevo (Sendinblue) — sending the verification email when you create your account and any other service message about your account. Only if you agree — when you create your account, or later in Settings — Brevo also sends you short tips about how to use the app (a welcome, gentle reminders and one email per feature) and, now and then, an email asking for your opinion of the app on the App Store or Google Play — giving it is up to you, and nothing is ever offered in return. Brevo receives your email address and the language you use the app in, so that every message reaches you written in your own language, and an identifier of your account that is used only in the link to unsubscribe. Nothing about your health is ever sent with them. You can withdraw your agreement at any time in Settings › «Tips & review requests by email» or with the link at the bottom of every email; the verification and account messages still arrive. Brevo also delivers to our team the notice of each new report (see «Reports and blocking» below), which carries no text written by members.
  • RevenueCat — subscription management.
  • Open Food Facts — when you scan a food barcode, your device looks it up directly in the Open Food Facts database, so your device's IP address and the scanned barcode reach their servers. No account or health data is sent with the lookup.
  • Translation of community posts — when you tap «Translate» under a post, the translation is made on your phone (Apple Translation on iPhone, Google ML Kit on Android), so the text of the post never leaves your device for that. The first time you choose a language, your phone downloads it from Apple or Google. On Android, Google ML Kit sends Google technical data about your device and the app (device model and system version, app version, per-installation identifiers, performance metrics and the languages you chose) — never the content of what is translated.
  • Apple / Google — app distribution and billing and, if you choose «Continue with Apple» or «Continue with Google», confirming who you are when you sign in (see Section 1). If you use Apple’s «Hide My Email», our emails reach you through Apple’s private email relay.
  • GitHub (Microsoft) — hosting of our websites and of the web version at chronicfriends.app. GitHub serves the page files to your browser, so it sees your IP address and basic request information. No account or health data is sent to GitHub: your data travels between your browser and Firebase, not through the hosting. The mobile app also checks there, each time it starts, whether there is an update to its content, and downloads from there the music of the relaxation tools; and the images in our emails are loaded from there when you open them. In those moments, GitHub sees your IP address too.
  • Google Workspace (Google) — our email. The messages you send to info@chronicfriends.org, and the notices of new reports, are received and stored in a Google Workspace mailbox that we manage. We read and answer your emails ourselves: they are not passed to any artificial-intelligence service.

Being found by other members. Other members can find you in the community and send you a friend request. To make that possible, your display name and avatar — the same ones you already show on your posts — are listed in a member directory, together with whether your account is a patient or a doctor account and the time you last had the app open, which is what shows other members whether you are online right now. Any signed-in, verified member sees the full list of members, without having to search; the search box is only a filter over that list. Your email address is never in it, and neither is anything about your health or the conditions you follow. This is switched on when you create your account, and you can switch it off whenever you like, in Settings → “Let others find me”: switching it off removes you from that list — you no longer appear in it, and nobody can reach you by browsing it or by searching for your name. Your existing friendships and conversations are unaffected. Nobody can send you a message without you having accepted them as a friend first.

Showing your details on your profile. Being listed in the member directory covers your display name, your avatar, whether yours is a patient or a doctor account, and when you last had the app open — that last one is what makes the green “online” dot next to a name mean something. It carries nothing else. Publishing anything more about yourself is a separate decision, with its own switch in Settings → “Show my details on my profile”, and that switch is off until you turn it on. Turning it on publishes up to four things on your profile, so that any signed-in, verified member who opens it can see them: your country, your age, the number of years you have lived with your condition, and the short bio you wrote about yourself. Only the fields you have actually filled in are published; anything you left empty simply does not appear. Because your age and the years you have lived with a condition say something about your health, we treat this as health data and we ask you for it separately and explicitly — being listed in the directory, which is on unless you switch it off, covers only your name and your photo, not this. These details are not part of the browsable member list: they sit on your own profile and are read one profile at a time. That happens when a member opens your profile, and it also happens in advance on the phones of your friends and of anyone you have sent a friend request to — when their app starts, it loads the profiles behind their friend list and their pending requests so that those cards are ready before they are opened, and a copy stays in that phone’s local storage. You can switch this off whenever you like, and when you do, those four fields are deleted from your published profile, not merely hidden. Your display name, your avatar, your friendships and your conversations are unaffected.

The steps tournament. If you join the steps tournament, the step totals described in Section 1 are visible to any signed-in, verified member, next to your display name and avatar. It happens only if you choose to take part, and leaving the tournament deletes your row. Apart from those totals and the profile details described just above, nothing else about your health is ever visible to other members: not your journal, not your check-ins, not your questionnaire scores, not a single day-by-day figure. What you decide to publish in the community is, of course, visible, including the condition you choose for a post. And two smaller things are visible too: your public profile includes the language you use the app in, and when you like a post, the like is saved with your account identifier.

Reports and blocking. If you report a post, a comment, a chat, a message or a member, or if you block a member, the app sends a report to our team so that we can review it. A report contains your account identifier, what was reported or blocked (its type and its identifier), the reason you chose, when it happened and, if you write one, your description. When you block a member, it also says where you blocked them from and their display name and, if you blocked them from a post or a comment — which are already public in the community — the beginning of its text. A private chat is never attached: only its identifier. Reports are stored in our database in Zurich and only our team can read them. The email that tells us about a new report carries only the type of report, the reason, the time, the identifiers and a link — never your description or any text from the community. We keep reports for 12 months, also after the accounts involved are deleted, so that we can deal with repeated abuse; after that, they are deleted.

We may also disclose data where the law requires it. We do not share your data with anyone else.

5. Where your data is stored, and international transfers

Your health data and the rest of your account data — your profile, journal, community activity and settings — are stored on Google Cloud / Firebase servers in Zurich, Switzerland (region europe-west6). Files you upload, such as a photo in a post, are stored in Frankfurt, Germany (region europe-west3). There is one exception: the sign-in service, Firebase Authentication, is run by Google in the United States. It keeps only what is needed to sign you in — your email address, your encrypted password or the identifier of your Apple or Google sign-in, the name and picture of your Google Account if you sign in with Google, and when you created your account and last signed in — and, to protect sign-in against abuse, it also uses the IP address and the type of device or browser you sign in from. No health data is ever stored there. Two of our server tasks — sending the verification email and handling the link to unsubscribe from our emails — run in Belgium (region europe-west1), inside the EU. We host in Europe on purpose: Switzerland is covered by a European Commission adequacy decision and applies the revFADP, one of the strictest data-protection laws anywhere, and the GDPR applies to how we handle your data regardless of where the servers sit. Both European storage locations are permanent and cannot be changed.

What stays only on your device: the resting heart rate and heart-rate variability that Health Sync reads on iPhone; your answers and scores in the self-assessment questionnaires; the photos in your Photo diary; and, for doctors, the professional details of a doctor account. They are not sent to Firebase and they do not reach our servers, so they do not come back on a new phone or another computer, and if you delete the app they go with it. Within Health Sync, your daily steps, sleep and activity minutes are different: they are saved in your journal and stored in your account, as described in Section 1. In the steps tournament, other members only ever see your four step totals, never a day-by-day figure.

On a computer. When you use the web version at chronicfriends.app, the same rules apply, with one addition: your browser keeps a working copy of your entries in its own local storage on that computer, so the app still opens if you lose connection. When you log out, the app asks whether to remove your data from that computer. If other people use that computer, choose «Yes»: the app first checks that everything has reached your account and then removes the copy. If Chronic Friends is open in other tabs or windows of that browser, close them first. If you choose «No», the copy stays on that computer, ready for the next time you sign in. Clearing your browser data also removes it. None of this deletes your account: your data is still there when you sign in again from your phone or another computer — except what is kept only on that computer, such as your Photo diary and your questionnaire scores, which is removed for good.

Some providers process certain data outside Switzerland and the EEA, mainly in the United States: Firebase Authentication (your sign-in details, see above), Firebase Crashlytics (error reports), RevenueCat (subscription status), GitHub (hosting our websites, the web version and the app’s content updates), Google ML Kit on Android (technical data when you translate a post), Apple / Google (billing, app distribution and, if you use it, signing in) and Google Workspace (the emails you send us). None of them receives the health data you log in the app. Where this happens, we rely on safeguards such as the EU–US and Swiss–US Data Privacy Framework or Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs).

6. Retention

We keep your data while your account is active. After you delete your account, we delete your data within 30 days, except where the law requires us to keep certain records (for example, billing). Backups rotate out in the normal course.

Private messages are deleted automatically from our servers 12 months after they are sent, even while your account stays active. The copies on your own devices stay there until you delete the app or, on a computer, until you remove your data when you log out.

If you signed in with Apple, deleting your account also asks Apple to disconnect Chronic Friends from your Apple ID. Removing Chronic Friends from your Apple ID or Google Account settings does not delete your data: to delete it, use «Delete my account» in Settings.

If you take part in the steps tournament, your row is deleted the moment you leave it, and in any case when you delete your account.

If you switched on “Show my details on my profile”, switching it off deletes your country, your age, your years with the condition and your bio from your published profile — they are removed from it, not hidden. The deletion is sent the moment you switch it off; if your device is offline, or if it cannot be sent right then, the app sends it again the next time you open it while signed in. If you delete your account, they are erased with the rest of your data, within the 30 days described above.

Reports sent to our team, including the ones that blocking a member sends, are kept for 12 months and then deleted, also if the accounts involved are deleted sooner (see Section 4). Your own list of the members you have blocked stays in your account until you unblock them or delete your account.

Error reports are kept by Firebase Crashlytics for 90 days, after which Google starts deleting them.

After you delete your account, a few records that contain no health data can remain: the billing records kept by Apple, Google and RevenueCat; a note that an account was suspended, if it was suspended for breaking the community rules; and our servers’ technical logs, which are deleted automatically after 30 days.

11. Medical disclaimer

Chronic Friends is a wellness, self-tracking, and community tool. It is not a medical device and is not a substitute for professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment.

  • The radar, body map, graphs, and trends visualize the data you enter yourself. They do not predict, diagnose, or detect any disease or medical condition.
  • Choosing which condition(s) you follow only personalizes which trackers you see. It is not a diagnosis, and Chronic Friends does not provide medical advice.
  • Any readings you type in from your own home devices (such as blood pressure, peak flow, or glucose) are shown only as a simple timeline of what you entered, with no reference ranges, no colour zones, no "high/low/normal" labels, and no alerts or interpretation.
  • Standardised questionnaires (such as the PHQ-9, the GAD-7, the AUDIT-C, or a condition activity index) are self-assessment tools, reproduced from their official published versions and offered only in the languages in which an official translation exists. The app shows you the score you obtained and does not interpret it: it assigns no severity category, no risk level, and no clinical threshold, it raises no alert, and no other feature of the app reads that score to change what you are shown. There is one exception, for your safety: in the PHQ-9, if you answer the question about thoughts of death or of hurting yourself with anything other than “not at all”, the app shows you straight away where to find help in your country. Nothing is sent to anyone. A score is not a diagnosis. It is there for you to discuss with your doctor.
  • This version of the app does not use artificial intelligence on your health data, and does not send it to any AI provider.
  • Always seek the advice of your physician or another qualified health professional with any questions about your condition, and never disregard professional medical advice because of something you saw in the app.
  • Online consultations with doctors are not available yet.
  • In an emergency, call your local emergency services.
