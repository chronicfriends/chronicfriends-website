Why Chronic Friends doesn't predict your flares

2026-07-28

Open any app store and look at what apps for chronic illness promise. Somewhere in the screenshots there is usually a number, a colour and a word: elevated risk, flare likely, 78%.

Chronic Friends doesn't do that. Our Flare Radar takes what you wrote down over the last few days and puts it in one picture. That's all it does. It doesn't score you, it doesn't warn you, and it doesn't tell you a flare is coming.

That was a decision, not an omission. Here's the reasoning, because we think you deserve it.

Nobody can predict your flare from a phone

A prediction is a claim about the future, and a claim needs evidence. What an app has is the handful of things you had the energy to log — some days carefully, some days not at all. Turning that into a percentage doesn't make it more true. It makes it look more true, which is worse.

And you already know the cost of a false alarm. A bad number on a Tuesday morning changes what you eat, whether you go to work, whether you call someone. That's a real consequence, produced by a guess.

The moment a score becomes advice, the app becomes something else

There's a line in European rules that most people never have to think about. Software that merely records and displays what you enter is one thing. Software that interprets it — that attaches a severity, a risk level, a threshold — starts to look like a medical device, with everything that implies.

We stay on the near side of that line on purpose. When Chronic Friends shows you a score from a standardised questionnaire, it shows you the number and stops. No label, no colour zone, no "moderate to severe". The number is yours, to take to the person qualified to interpret it.

What we do instead

We think the useful thing was never the prediction. It's arriving at your appointment able to say what actually happened — not "it's been bad lately", but six weeks of your own days, in front of you both. Most people can't hold that in their head. Nobody should have to.

So the app is built for that: twenty seconds a day, your own patterns over time, and a report you can hand over. What it means is a conversation between you and your doctor. We're not in that conversation, and we shouldn't be.

The honest version of the promise

We can't tell you when your next bad day is coming. What we can do is make sure that when it arrives, you're not explaining it from memory, and you're not explaining it alone.
